## Metric definitions

**Meaningfulness** — Does this triple express a coherent and plausible commonsense relation?
This metric captures whether the subject and object have a genuine, conceivable semantic connection through this predicate. It is NOT just about grammar — a grammatically correct but semantically absurd combination should score low.
- 0: Incoherent. The subject and object have no conceivable connection through this predicate. (banana, HasProperty, democratic)
- 1: A human can parse the words but the combination is absurd or nonsensical. (benzene, AtLocation, CNN)
- 2: Marginally conceivable but very forced — requires inventing a far-fetched scenario.
- 3: Understandable but vague, overly generic, or the connection is weak.
- 4: Clear and coherent commonsense assertion with minor ambiguity.
- 5: Immediately clear and plausible. Any human would understand the claim and find it reasonable to evaluate.

**Typicality** — Is the stated relation generally true?
This metric captures how broadly the relation holds across instances of the subject.
A rare but possible case should score low (1-2), not high — reserve high scores for relations that hold for the clear majority of instances.
- 0: False or essentially never true.
- 1: True only in rare or contrived cases.
- 2: True for a minority of instances.
- 3: True for roughly half of instances, or debatable.
- 4: True for most instances, with some exceptions.
- 5: True for virtually all instances; universal or near-universal.

**Saliency** — Would humans spontaneously mention this when describing the subject?
This metric captures whether the relation is among the most prominent, defining, or noteworthy traits of the subject.
- 0: No one would think of this when asked about the subject.
- 1: Very unlikely to come to mind; trivial or obscure.
- 2: Might come to mind after thought, but not notable.
- 3: Reasonably notable; would appear in a longer description.
- 4: Among the important traits; many people would mention it.
- 5: One of the first things that comes to mind; a defining trait.

## Scoring rules

1. **Scores must match your reasoning.** If your reasoning says "unlikely", "not typical", "not associated", or "does not", the corresponding score MUST be low (0-2). Never write a negative justification with a high score.
2. **Do not invent facts.** If you are not sure whether a factual claim is true, score Typicality low. Do not fabricate justifications.
3. **Meaningfulness gates the other scores.** If Meaningfulness ≤ 2, then Typicality and Saliency must also be ≤ 2.
4. **Be strict with 5s.** A score of 5 means you are fully confident. If there is any hesitation, use 4 or lower.
5. **Watch for tautologies.** If Subject and Object are identical, synonymous, or the Object simply repeats a word from the Subject, Saliency and Meaningfulness should score low (0-1).
